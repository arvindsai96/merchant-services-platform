Interchange-Plus vs Tiered Pricing: How to Stop Overpaying on Merchant Fees

Olivia Carter
Senior Fintech Pricing Analyst
5 min read 4,820 views

Detailed financial audit comparing Interchange-Plus wholesale card brand pass-through rates against opaque tiered buckets (Qualified, Mid-Qualified, Non-Qualified).

If your monthly merchant statement groups card volume into "Qualified", "Mid-Qualified", and "Non-Qualified" buckets, your payment processor is likely charging you profit margins up to 40% higher than the true wholesale interchange rates established by Visa and Mastercard.

In merchant services, few fee structures are as pervasive—and as inherently biased against the merchant—as tiered pricing. Understanding how these tiers work and migrating to transparent Interchange-Plus pricing can directly inject thousands of dollars back into your operating cash flow every month.

"By eliminating tiered downgrade penalties and moving to true Interchange-Plus, average retail merchants doing $100k/month save between 24% and 38% on gross card fees—equivalent to $14,000 to $28,000 in recovered annual profit."

1. Anatomy of a Credit Card Transaction: Who Gets Paid?

Every time a customer swipes, dips, or taps their card at your counter or web checkout, the processing fee is split into three fundamental components:

1. Interchange Fee

Paid directly to the cardholder's issuing bank (Chase, Citi, BofA). Non-negotiable and set by card networks twice a year.

2. Assessment Fee

Paid directly to the card brands (Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover). Typically ~0.14% plus transaction network communication costs.

3. Processor Markup

The only fee controlled by your merchant provider. With Interchange-Plus, this is fixed. With tiered pricing, it is arbitrarily expanded.

2. How Tiered Pricing Exploits Downgrades

In a tiered pricing contract, the processor groups cards into three buckets: Qualified (lowest rate, e.g., 1.79%), Mid-Qualified (e.g., 2.65%), and Non-Qualified (e.g., 3.85% + $0.30).

While processors advertise the low "Qualified" rate to win your business, today's consumer market is dominated by cash-back cards, airline points cards, and corporate credit cards. The processor's underwriting algorithm automatically flags these cards as "Non-Qualified", charging you an exorbitant markup that stays entirely in the processor's pockets.

4. Actionable Steps to Switch and Audit Your Statement

  1. Calculate Your Effective Rate: Take total fees deducted last month and divide by gross card volume. If it exceeds 2.4% for retail or 2.7% for e-commerce, you are overpaying.
  2. Examine the Line Items: Search for terms like "Non-Qual Surchg", "EIRF", or "Downgrade". These indicate arbitrary penalty tiers.
  3. Demand Unbundled Interchange-Plus: Require your provider to itemize exact card brand interchange pass-through with a transparent fixed markup and zero cancellation fees.

Understanding Durbin Amendment Debit Interchange Caps: How to Stop Leaving Thousands on the Table

David Lewis
Finance Director
4 min read Federal Reserve Regulation

Federal Reserve Durbin Amendment cap analytics showing statutory debit cost basis (21¢ + 0.05% + 1¢ fraud adjustment) vs. unregulated issuer markups.

Over 50% of all consumer transactions in the United States are settled using debit cards. Yet most business owners do not realize that the Federal Reserve caps the wholesale interchange fee on the majority of these cards at just a fraction of a percent.

Passed under the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform Act, the Durbin Amendment established strict limits on debit interchange fees charged by large national banks. If your business is on flat-rate or tiered pricing, your processor is likely keeping this statutory savings rather than passing it to your bank account.

"On a $100 regulated debit swipe, the true wholesale fee is exactly 27 cents (0.27%). If your processor charges you a flat 2.6% + 10¢ ($2.70), they are pocketing a 900% profit margin on that transaction."

1. The Statutory Durbin Formula: Regulated vs. Exempt Banks

The Durbin Amendment classifies card issuing banks into two distinct regulatory groups based on their total consolidated asset size:

Regulated Issuers (> $10 Billion Assets)

Includes major national banks: JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Citibank, Capital One.

Cap: 21¢ + 0.05% of sale + 1¢ fraud mitigation
Exempt Issuers (< $10 Billion Assets)

Includes community banks, local credit unions, and municipal financial institutions.

Market-Driven: Typically ~1.15% to 1.60% + $0.15

3. How to Unlock True Regulated Debit Pass-Through

To ensure your business receives 100% of the Durbin savings:

  1. Verify PIN Debit Routing: Enable least-cost debit network routing (Star, NYCE, Pulse, Accel) to automatically bypass Visa/Mastercard higher rails when available.
  2. Insist on Unbundled Interchange-Plus Billing: When debit is passed through at cost, your effective rate on debit transactions drops below 0.60%.
